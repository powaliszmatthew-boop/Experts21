# 9. Biggest Risks

Ranked by likelihood × impact (our assessment).

| # | Risk | Evidence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High rent and losing the lease** | Average retail asking rent of $50.27/sf, Brickell $84.96/sf (Q3 2026); vacancy only 3.1%. Panther Coffee lost its Sunset Harbour lease (closed Feb 2026); Suite Habana lost its Wynwood lease (Apr 2026). An operator quoted by CoStar: "can't make money" at $250–300/sf. | Keep rent under about 10–12% of projected sales. Get a long initial term with renewal options, capped escalators, a TI allowance and free rent during permitting. Have an attorney review the lease. Consider secondary streets and suburbs. |
| 2 | **Running out of cash before the ramp-up** | Permitting takes "several months"; startup cost is about $226k–$568k (estimate). First-year closure estimates for restaurants run about 10–26% depending on the study. | Hold at least 6 months of working capital. Pre-sell through a waitlist and soft opening. Phase capital spending. |
| 3 | **Labor cost inflation** | Minimum wage at $15 since Sept 30, 2026, CPI-indexed from 2027. | Efficient layout and equipment (batch brew, automated milk), smart scheduling, menu prices reviewed yearly. |
| 4 | **Competition and price anchoring** | Cuban cafeterias set a low espresso price. Chains are expanding (Paris Baguette targeting 40–50 South Florida sites; drive-thrus Cali Coffee and 7 Brew); 55% of out-of-home buyers use drive-thrus. | Differentiate on quality and local identity; offer a value cafecito; build app ordering and a walk-up window; build loyalty. |
| 5 | **Permitting delays and cost overruns** | CU only after construction; DERM grease (FOG) requirements; impact fees; FOG rules under county review in 2026. | Choose a second-generation food space; hire an expediter or architect experienced with City of Miami and DERM; make the lease contingent on zoning. |
| 6 | **Hurricanes, flooding and insurance** | Hurricane season runs June 1–Nov 30. Florida insurance costs remain among the highest in the US, though surplus-lines property cost fell 35% in H1 2026. | Business interruption, flood and windstorm cover; check FEMA flood zones before choosing a site; a generator or backup plan; hurricane shutters; an inventory protection plan. |
| 7 | **Coffee price volatility** | Arabica hit a record of about $4.40/lb (Feb 2025) and was about $2.9/lb by Oct 2026. Possible El Niño risk to Brazil's 2027/28 crop. Tariff policy has changed several times. | Fixed-price contracts with a roaster; diversify origins; keep a margin buffer in menu prices. |
| 8 | **Seasonality and the tourist mix** | About 28M visitors a year, concentrated in winter and spring; summer heat lowers foot traffic. | Base the plan on residents; push iced drinks in summer; wholesale and catering for steady revenue. |
| 9 | **Regulatory and tax surprises** | Adding alcohol triggers the county 1% food and beverage tax; Miami Beach has its own resort tax; each municipality has different rules. | Confirm the jurisdiction early; get tax and legal advice before adding beer and wine. |
| 10 | **Staffing and turnover** | A tight hospitality labor market; bilingual skills needed. | Wage ladder, tips, training and culture; cross-train staff. |
