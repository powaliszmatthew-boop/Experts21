# 5. Operating Costs

## Rent (Reported: Cushman & Wakefield MarketBeat)

| Area | Asking rent (NNN, $/sf/yr) | Vacancy | Period |
|---|---|---|---|
| Miami-Dade average retail | **$50.27** (+1.8% YoY) | 3.1% | Q3 2026 |
| Brickell | **$84.96** (−2.4% YoY) | — | Q3 2026 |
| Wynwood / Design District | ~$69.88 | 7.9% | late 2025 |
| Coral Gables | ~$56.61 | 1.3% | late 2025 |

- Add about **$2–$4/sf for CAM, taxes and insurance**, typical for NNN leases (broker guide).
- Low vacancy gives landlords leverage, so expect personal guarantees, escalators of about 3%/yr, and competition for second-generation spaces.
- Florida no longer charges **sales tax on commercial rent** (from Oct 1, 2025).

## Labor (Verified: Florida minimum wage schedule)

- **$15.00/hr** minimum since **Sept 30, 2026**. The tipped cash wage is **$11.98** (maximum tip credit $3.02).
- From Sept 30, 2027, the wage is **indexed annually to inflation**.
- Reported local pay for fast-food and counter workers (BLS SOC 35-3023, which includes baristas), May 2025 via a third party: median about $14.23, mean about $15.27, before tips. These figures predate the $15 floor, so the market rate now starts at $15 or more.
- Add employer payroll taxes (FICA 7.65%, FUTA/SUTA) and workers' comp, roughly 10–15% on top of wages (estimate).

## Cost of goods (Reported benchmarks)

- **COGS: 25–35%** of sales in US guides, with consultants targeting under 30%. Australian tax-office benchmarks run 34–42%.
- **Labor: 25–32%** of sales targets.
- **Average ticket: $6–$12**, depending on location and food attach rate.
- **Net margin: 15–25%** is commonly cited (Toast/UpMenu). Treat this as optimistic.
- Green coffee prices fell more than 20% in 2026, but retail prices lag. Price from your own per-cup cost.

## Taxes collected or owed

- **Sales tax: 7%** in Miami-Dade (6% state + 1% county surtax). This is collected from customers. Register with Florida DOR (Form DR-1).
- **County 1% food and beverage tax** applies to food and drinks consumed on premises at certain establishments **with a liquor license** (funds the Homeless Trust and domestic violence centers). This matters only if we add beer and wine. Miami Beach, Surfside and Bal Harbour are exempt from it but levy their own **resort tax** (Miami Beach: 2% on food and beverage).
- Annual local business tax receipts (city + county) and the DBPR license renewal. See [06-permits-licenses.md](06-permits-licenses.md).

## Insurance (Reported)

- Broker estimate: **$3,000–$15,000/yr** for a Florida restaurant package. Unverified, and it doesn't separate property from liability.
- Florida surplus-lines commercial property cost per policy was **down 35% in H1 2026** compared with H1 2025, and wind/hail was down 28%. The market is easing but premiums remain among the highest in the US.
- Required or typical policies: general liability, property including windstorm, flood (if in a flood zone), workers' comp (required at 4+ employees), business interruption.

## Illustrative monthly P&L (Estimate)

Assumptions: 1,200 sf; rent $64/sf all-in; 200 transactions/day × $7.50 × 30 days; the owner acts as manager (no salary in this model).

| Line | $/month | % of sales |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue** | **45,000** | 100% |
| COGS (30%) | 13,500 | 30.0% |
| Hourly labor (810 hrs × $16) + about 12% payroll tax/WC | 14,515 | 32.3% |
| Rent + CAM | 6,400 | 14.2% |
| Utilities | 1,500 | 3.3% |
| Card processing (~2.75%) | 1,238 | 2.8% |
| Marketing | 1,000 | 2.2% |
| Insurance | 750 | 1.7% |
| Supplies, cleaning, smallwares | 600 | 1.3% |
| Repairs and maintenance | 500 | 1.1% |
| POS, app and software | 400 | 0.9% |
| Accounting and legal | 400 | 0.9% |
| Waste, pest control, licenses | 400 | 0.9% |
| **Operating profit (before debt service, depreciation, owner pay)** | **≈ 3,800** | **≈ 8.4%** |

**Break-even** (fixed costs of about $26.5k/mo; variable COGS + card fees of about 32.75%): about **$39.4k/mo ≈ $1,310/day ≈ 175 transactions/day** at $7.50.
At Brickell rents (about $89/sf all-in), break-even rises to about **$43.1k/mo ≈ 191 transactions/day**.

Takeaway: margins are thin. A paid general manager (about $5k/mo) would wipe out the profit at this volume. Three things move the result most: **ticket size (food attach rate), rent per square foot, and labor scheduling**.
