What this means: the market is large, bilingual and mostly Hispanic, and a quarter of household income goes to housing costs. Customers are sensitive to price, while some neighborhoods are very affluent. Plan bilingual (English/Spanish) signage, menus and staff.

## Tourism (Reported: GMCVB via press)

- About **28 million visitors in 2025**. One report gives 28.3M, and 2024 was also above 28M, a record at the time.
- **$23B** in visitor spending in 2025, up 4% from 2024.
- Top international overnight markets: Colombia, Canada, Brazil, the UK and Mexico. Top domestic markets: New York, DC, Boston, Chicago and Atlanta.
- Tourist foot traffic is concentrated in Miami Beach, Brickell/Downtown, Wynwood and Little Havana. Note that the **2026 FIFA World Cup** brought matches to Miami in summer 2026, so 2026 traffic may be unusually high.

## US specialty coffee demand (Reported: NCA / SCA National Coffee Data Trends)

- **46%** of US adults drank specialty coffee in the past day (Jan 2025 survey), up 84% since 2011 and ahead of traditional coffee (42%) for the first time.
- **47%** in the 2026 report, holding at a record high.
- 36% of past-day specialty drinkers had it prepared **away from home**, compared with 23% of traditional drinkers.
- Specialty drinkers average **3.0 cups/day**, against 2.0 for all drinkers.
- We found no reliable dollar figure for the US specialty market. NCA's full data is paid.

## Coffee commodity backdrop (Reported)

- Arabica futures hit a record of about **$4.40–4.41/lb in Feb 2025**. They were about **$2.86 (Sept 29, 2026)** and $2.95 (early Oct 2026) after a record Brazil crop.
- Coffee was **exempted from US tariffs on Brazil** (executive order effective Nov 13, 2025). The July 22, 2026 Section 301 action on Brazil also exempts coffee. Instant coffee's status is conflicting; check the Federal Register.
- Risk: analysts flag a possible "super" El Niño affecting Brazil's 2027/28 crop.
