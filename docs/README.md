# Miami Specialty Coffee Shop — Research Index

Background research for opening an independent specialty coffee shop in Miami, Florida. It is the shared context for the expert-agent team.

**Research date:** November 23, 2026. Figures change, so recheck anything time-sensitive (fees, wages, rents, coffee prices) before relying on it.

## How to read these docs

Each file sorts its claims into three levels:

- **Verified:** taken from a primary or official source (Census, DBPR, Florida CFO, a city or county site), and cited.
- **Reported:** from secondary sources such as news outlets, brokers or industry blogs, and cited. Treat these as directional.
- **Estimate / assumption:** our own synthesis or modeling, and labeled as such. Replace these with real quotes.

## Key takeaways

1. **Demand is real and rising.** 46–47% of US adults drank specialty coffee in the past day, a record (NCA 2025/2026). Miami-Dade has about 2.8M residents and about 28M visitors a year.
2. **Miami's coffee culture is Cuban-first.** 70.6% of residents are Hispanic or Latino and 54.5% are foreign-born. Cafecito and ventanita habits set price expectations, so a specialty shop has to fit in with that culture rather than ignore it.
3. **Rent is the biggest risk you can control.** The county average retail asking rent is about $50/sf, and Brickell is about $85/sf (Q3 2026). Well-known cafés (Panther Sunset Harbour, Suite Habana Wynwood) lost leases in 2026.
4. **Labor costs only go up.** Florida's minimum wage reached $15/hr on Sept 30, 2026, and is indexed to inflation from 2027.
5. **Green coffee costs eased in 2026.** Arabica fell from a record of about $4.40/lb (Feb 2025) to about $2.86–2.95/lb (Sept/Oct 2026). Coffee is exempt from the Brazil tariffs. The price is still volatile, with El Niño risk to Brazil's 2027/28 crop.
6. **Permits take months.** In the City of Miami, the Certificate of Use comes only after construction, and the BTR can take "several months." Budget time and rent for this.
7. **Convenience is winning nationally.** 40% of out-of-home coffee buyers order by app, and 55% use a drive-thru (NCA 2026). Plan mobile ordering from day one.

## Open questions for the expert team

- Neighborhood and site choice (Wynwood/Design District, Brickell, Coral Gables, Little Havana, MiMo, Coconut Grove, suburban).
- Concept: espresso bar vs. café with food vs. roaster-café; whether to add beer and wine (this triggers state alcohol licensing and the county 1% food and beverage tax).
- Format: walk-up window (ventanita) or drive-thru options, and seat count (this sets license tiers and occupant-load rules).
- Funding: total capital and how much working-capital runway to hold.
