# Source verification

Every factual claim you give the consultant must be traceable and labeled.

## Labels (same convention as `docs/`)
- **Verified**: from a primary or official source (Census, BLS, DBPR, Florida DOR/CFO, Florida Statutes, City of Miami, Miami-Dade County). Cite the URL.
- **Reported**: from secondary sources (news, brokers, vendors, industry blogs, NCA/SCA press summaries). Cite the URL.
- **Estimate**: your own synthesis, assumption, or model output. Say what it depends on.

## Method
1. Start from the research in `docs/` (index: `docs/README.md`). Note its research date (Nov 23, 2026).
2. For anything time-sensitive (fees, wages, rents, coffee prices, tax rules, openings and closures), check it is still current with WebSearch/WebFetch before relying on it.
3. Prefer the official source over a summary of it. When sources conflict, report both and say which you trust and why.
4. Give the period for every number ("Q3 2026", "2020-2024 ACS").
5. Never invent a figure, fee, citation, or quote. If you can't verify something, say so and list it as an open question.
6. Watch jurisdiction: City of Miami, Miami Beach, Coral Gables, and unincorporated Miami-Dade have different rules.

## Output
End every answer with a **Sources** list of markdown links. Mark any item that came from `docs/` rather than a fresh check as "(from docs/NN)".
